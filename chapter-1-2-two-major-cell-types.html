Section 1

An Evolutionary Relationship

Prokaryotes were the first organisms on Earth. There is strong evidence that eukaryotes evolved from prokaryotes — supported by the shared features they still carry today and the fossil record.

3.5 Billion
years ago
Oldest prokaryotes discovered in fossil records — the first living organisms on Earth.
2 Billion
years ago
Oldest unicellular eukaryotes appear in fossil records — 1.5 billion years after prokaryotes.
Evidence of
shared origin
Both cell types share cell membranes, nucleic acids (DNA & RNA), ribosomes and proteins — reflecting their common evolutionary past.
🦠 Prokaryotic Cells
Smaller, simpler, less specialised. No true nucleus — DNA resides in the cytoplasm (nucleoid region). No membrane-bound organelles. Examples: bacteria and archaea.

Despite their simplicity, prokaryotes possess all chemicals and processes needed to carry out life's processes. Their collective mass is considered many times that of all eukaryotes.
🔬 Eukaryotic Cells
Larger, more complex, highly compartmentalised. DNA stored in a nucleus. Contain many membrane-bound organelles. Examples: protists, fungi, plants and animals.

Compartmentalisation allows different environments within a single cell — enabling numerous metabolic reactions to occur simultaneously.
Section 2

Prokaryotic Cells

Unicellular cells approximately 0.1–5 μm in size. May be rod-shaped (bacillus), spherical (coccus), or spiral. DNA is a single circular chromosome located in the nucleoid region of the cytoplasm — there is no nucleus.

🛡️
Cell Wall
Prokaryote · Always present
Contains peptidoglycan. Strengthens the cell and maintains its shape. Surrounds the cell membrane.
🔵
Cell Surface Membrane
All cells · Always present
Controls the entry and exit of materials to and from the cell. Facilitates communication between cells.
🧫
Cytoplasm
All cells · Always present
Contains the cytosol (fluid). Provides a medium suitable for biochemical reactions. In prokaryotes, all cellular components float here — there is no nucleus.
🧬
Circular Chromosome (DNA)
Prokaryote · Always present
A single circular chromosome resides freely in the nucleoid region of the cytoplasm. Stores the cell's genetic information.
⚙️
Ribosomes
All cells · Always present
Smaller ribosomes than eukaryotes. Free-floating in the cytoplasm. Site of protein synthesis.
🫧
Capsule
Sometimes present
A layer of sticky material covering the cell wall. Used for protection and sticking to surfaces.
🪱
Pili (singular: pilus)
Sometimes present
Hair-like structures projecting from the cell wall. Used for attachment to other cells or surfaces.
🌀
Flagella (singular: flagellum)
Sometimes present
Filaments attached to the cell wall. Rotate to propel the bacterium — used for movement.
💾
Plasmids
Sometimes present
Small, separate loops of DNA in the cytoplasm. Independent of the main chromosome. Can carry useful genes (e.g. antibiotic resistance).
Section 3

Eukaryotic Cells

Generally 10–100 μm in size. DNA is stored as pairs of linear chromosomes inside a nucleus. Many membrane-bound organelles allow compartmentalised, simultaneous metabolic reactions. Found in protists, fungi, plants and animals.

🔑 Why Compartmentalisation Matters
The membrane-bound organelles of eukaryotic cells create separate internal environments. This means the cell can maintain different conditions in different compartments simultaneously — allowing many more types of metabolic reactions than a prokaryote can manage. This is why eukaryotic cells can grow much larger and specialise far more.
Section 4

Eukaryotic Structures & Functions

The following structures are found in eukaryotic cells. Note which are found in all eukaryotes and which are plant- or animal-specific.

🔵
Cell Membrane
All cells
Controls the exchange of materials between the cell and its external environment. Facilitates communication between cells. Not a membrane-bound organelle.
🧫
Cytoplasm
All cells
Contains the cytosol — provides a medium for biochemical reactions. Houses all internal organelles. Not considered an organelle itself.
⚫
Nucleus
All eukaryotes
Stores and protects genetic information (DNA). Coordinates overall cell function. Contains a nucleolus inside it. Houses linear chromosomes in pairs. Largest organelle (apart from central vacuole in plants).
⚡
Mitochondrion
All eukaryotes
Carries out aerobic respiration to produce ATP — the cell's energy-carrying molecule. Has inner membrane folds called cristae and an outer membrane.
🟢
Chloroplast
Plant cells only
Carries out photosynthesis — produces glucose from CO₂ and H₂O using light energy. Contains grana (stacks of membrane with chlorophyll), a stroma, and inner/outer membranes.
🧱
Cell Wall
Plant & fungi only
Strengthens the cell and maintains its shape. In plants: made of cellulose. In fungi: made of chitin. Not present in animal cells. Not an internal membrane-bound organelle.
💧
Large Central Vacuole
Plant cells only
Multiple functions: water storage, structural support & regulating cell shape, nutrient storage, and storage/disposal of waste products. Animal cells have small vacuoles only.
🔴
Rough ER (RER)
Eukaryotes
Packages proteins made by ribosomes into small vacuoles for transport to the Golgi body. Studded with ribosomes — hence "rough".
🟠
Smooth ER (SER)
Eukaryotes
Synthesises lipids (e.g. phospholipids and cholesterol) and metabolises carbohydrates. No ribosomes — hence "smooth".
📦
Golgi Body (Golgi Apparatus)
Eukaryotes
Modification, processing and packaging of proteins into vesicles for storage in the cell or secretion outside the cell by exocytosis.
⚙️
Ribosomes
All cells
Larger than prokaryotic ribosomes. Generally attached to the RER, but can also be free-floating. Site of protein synthesis for the cell or for secretion.
🔧
Centrioles
Animal cells only
Required for the development of the spindle apparatus during cell division — used to separate sister chromatids. Not found in plant cells.
Section 5

Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

Both are eukaryotic, but their structures differ in important ways. To confirm a cell is a plant cell, look for the three key plant-specific features below.

Structure 🌿 Plant Cell 🐾 Animal Cell
Cell Wall ✓ Present — made of cellulose ✗ Absent
Large Central Vacuole ✓ Present — large and central ✗ Absent (small vacuoles only)
Chloroplasts ✓ Present — in photosynthetic cells ✗ Absent
Centrioles ✗ Absent ✓ Present
Nucleus ✓ Present ✓ Present
Mitochondria ✓ Present ✓ Present
Ribosomes ✓ Present ✓ Present
RER / SER / Golgi ✓ Present ✓ Present
Cell Membrane ✓ Present ✓ Present
⚠️ Don't Confuse Chloroplasts with Mitochondria
Both are similar in shape and size, but differ in structure and function. Chloroplasts contain grana (visible stacks) and carry out photosynthesis. Mitochondria have internal cristae (folded inner membrane lines) and carry out aerobic respiration. Only chloroplasts are exclusive to plant cells — if a cell contains chloroplasts, it is a plant cell.
Section 6

Identifying Organelles Under a Microscope

Three key organelles have distinctive internal structures that allow them to be identified in electron micrographs.

⚫
Nucleus
KEY IDENTIFIER: Nucleolus
The largest organelle in the cell. Contains a clearly visible nucleolus inside. Surrounded by the nuclear membrane with nuclear pores.
🟢
Chloroplast
KEY IDENTIFIER: Grana (stacks)
Clearly visible grana — stacks of membrane containing chlorophyll. These "stacks" are distinct from the "lines" of mitochondria.
⚡
Mitochondrion
KEY IDENTIFIER: Cristae (lines)
Folded inner membrane extensions called cristae appear as lines across the organelle. These "lines" contrast clearly with the "stacks" of chloroplasts.

A structural and functional comparison of the two major cell types.

Chromosome Shape Single, circular chromosome Many linear chromosomes (in pairs)

🌍 Shared Features = Shared Evolutionary Origin
The fact that both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells share cell membranes, nucleic acids (DNA and RNA), ribosomes and proteins provides strong evidence they share a common evolutionary ancestor — despite their significant structural differences.
